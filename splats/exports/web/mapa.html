Terrain & water — how the land shapes the water 14

?
Where this data comes from

DEM: Copernicus GLO-30 v1 — 30 m global DEM, mosaiced from 2 tiles covering the 10 km box, reprojected to EPSG:4326 in Copernicus' standard resample.

Hydrology derivation (Python pipeline in scripts/derive_hydro.py):

  • Flow direction via D8 (steepest-descent neighbour)
  • Flow accumulation via weighted-flow
  • Threshold classification: main rivers ≥ 25 km² (42 segments) · tributaries 5–25 km² (328) · creeks 1–5 km² (1,342)

Hillshade: Horn (1981) formula, 315° azimuth · 45° altitude, computed from the 30 m DEM at 2 m/pixel. The district-scale variant covers the full Escobar District with a clipped 30 m DEM.

Wetlands audit: MapBiomas class 11 + 12 polygons were hand-checked against Sentinel-2 NIR composites — see data/surface_water_10km.geojson verdict field for the per-polygon decision.

Land cover & forest change 5

?
Where this data comes from

NDVI: Sentinel-2 L2A surface reflectance, bands B04 + B08, June 2026 composite, clouds < 20% (mosaic of 2 MGRS tiles). NDVI = (NIR−RED)/(NIR+RED). 0=non-vegetation, 1=lush canopy.

MapBiomas Paraguay Collection 2 (2023): categorical land cover at 30 m. Class 3 = upland Atlantic Forest, class 6 = flooded/gallery forest, class 11 = wetland, class 12 = grassland, class 15 = pasture, class 18 = agriculture, class 22 = non-vegetated, class 26 = open water. 10 classes observed in the 10 km box.

Hansen GFC v1.12: global forest loss (2001–2024, 30 m, 0=stable 1=loss) and gain (2000–2012). Each pixel records whether year ≤ 2024 had a canopy disturbance / regrowth event.

At the LQV parcel scale, forest loss over the 39-year observation window is effectively zero — that's the headline finding.

Each layer covers the full 10 km box. The combined story at the LQV parcel: stable forest (39-yr Hansen) + open-canopy Atlantic Forest (MapBiomas class 3) + NDVI 0.45–0.60 (Sentinel-2).

OSM context — what's around 6

?
Where this data comes from

Source: OpenStreetMap via the Overpass API. Snapshot: 2026-07-05. ~30,166 features in the 10 km box.

Per-feature provenance: every line/polygon carries the OSM osm_id, name (when set by a mapper), and the original tag set (highway=, building=, landuse=, etc.). You can verify any feature directly in the OSM iD editor.

Woodland-merged is a derived layer that combines all four forest sources (MapBiomas + Hansen + OSM + NDVI) into a single 5,510-polygon canopy. Useful for buyers who want a "one toggle = all forest" view.

The numbers in the layer rows (e.g. 999 roads) are computed live as data loads; hover any layer to see how many of its features fall inside the LQV parcel.

About this map

Property perimeter from Wesley's on-the-ground GPS walk (Guru Maps iOS, 06-22 + 06-28): 17 corners, walked in order, 4.26 km perimeter, 71.4 ha projected. Handheld GPS accuracy ±3–5 m per point — not survey-grade.

Timestamps recovered from the live Guru Maps share (KML · 20 timestamps). Two sessions: 5 pts on 06-22 (north ridge), 15 pts on 06-28 (full perimeter). Hit ▶ Replay walk above to watch the perimeter being captured in time order.

10 km box (-57.13 / -56.93 lon, -25.70 / -25.52 lat) centred on the GPS centroid. Built from:
· Sentinel-2 L2A NDVI (mosaic of 2 MGRS tiles)
· Copernicus GLO-30 DEM + D8 flow accumulation
· JRC Global Surface Water 1984–2024
· MapBiomas Paraguay Collection 2 (2023)
· Hansen GFC v1.12 forest loss / gain
· OpenStreetMap Overpass (2026-07-05): 30,166 features

Escobar-District box (-57.22 / -57.00 lon, -25.78 / -25.46 lat) covers the full district of Escobar, Paraguarí. Same Copernicus GLO-30 DEM, clipped to the district extent. 2 new layers (Escobar-wide hillshade + Escobar colour-relief) live in the Terrain section.

Layer authoring tools:
· scripts/build_escobar_hillshade.py — produces the district-scale hillshade + color-relief from a clipped Copernicus DEM.
· scripts/derive_hydro.py — D8 flow accumulation, contour extraction, quebrada stream classification, HAND.
· scripts/build_viewer_data.py — orchestrates everything into data/ for the web viewer.

Icons courtesy of Guru Maps — the same bookmark pins Wes uses in his iOS app appear here so the map reads identically to his phone view.
