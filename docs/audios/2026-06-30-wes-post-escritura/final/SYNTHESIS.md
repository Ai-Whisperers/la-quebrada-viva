## 2. The four dreams (D1-D4, identity + structure)

### D1 — The name ("Riverstone Valley" is the working title)
Wes floated 5 candidates: **Riverstone Valley** (his first instinct,
"stolen from Yellowstone"), Villa del Cielo, Cielo Azul, Lluvia Dorada,
Lluvia de Oro (the German town visited). He leans toward something
"mooi Spaans" but isn't decided. The name drives URL, brand, voice —
and **blocks Phase 1 marketing**. Decision needed by W0.6.

### D2 — The 4-BV corporate structure (decided in shape)
Wes has designed the cascade himself:
- **BV-PY-1 (land)**: owns the 62 ha
- **BV-NL (finance)**: the Dutch holding, receives NL investment
- **BV-PY-2 (Fase 1 ops)**: builds + operates the first cabins
- **BV-PY-3 (Fase 2-3 ops)**: expansion, future cabins

Why this matters: NL BV > IB threshold is €70k. With €70k+ income, the
NL BV route is cheaper than Wes's personal IB. The 4-BV also lets him
sell machines from Fase 1 to Fase 2 to repay early investors (the
"machinepark rouleert" idea). **Legal validation** is W0.1 (NL+PY
dual-tax attorney).

### D3 — The partner roster
- **Thijs** = 25% co-owner, operations partner, on-site periodically
- **Sonja** = cultural + worker + price authority (canonical per Wes Rule 5)
- **Kiki** = NL+PY network connector
- **First investors** = 3-5 from Wes's NL network + Sonja's PY network
- **Construction crew** = Sonja-recruited, 5-8 men, 36-month build

### D4 — The build philosophy
- **Open community, not gated** (R04: minimal security, gate + camera only)
- **Native species reforestation** (D4 Wes priority: Kuikopee Dutch forester)
- **Low-impact cabins** that disappear into the landscape (Rule 1: landscape-first)
- **No imported furniture** — use what the land gives (lapacho timber, bamboo)
- **Fire-safety first** — 82% canopy cover, dry season is real (R01 fire plan)
