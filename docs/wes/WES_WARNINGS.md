# ⚠️ Things That Might Surprise You

> **For Wesley + non-technical newcomers.** A practical warning list of
> the non-obvious things that **will** trip you up if you don't know
> about them in advance. Reading this 5 minutes saves 5 days of pain.
>
> **Last updated:** 2026-07-03

---

## 1. Currency math is upside down

**1 USD = ~7,500 PYG (Paraguayan Guaraní).**

If you see a price like "₲ 2.250.000", that's **300 USD**, not 2.25
million. Paraguayan prices look enormous in numbers because the unit
is so small.

Most prices in the repo are quoted in **USD-equivalent** for clarity.
When the actual transaction happens, multiply by 7,500.

**Practical impact:** A €10,000 solar PV install is ₲ 75,000,000 in
guaraníes. Same money, different zeros.

## 2. Distance is measured in hours, not km

Paraguay's roads are slow. The 62-ha parcel is:

- **2 hours from Asunción** (the airport)
- **15 minutes from San Bernardino** (the nearest town)
- **5 minutes from a dirt road** (the last km is unpaved)

A "30 km" drive in PY often takes 45-60 minutes. Plan road trips
generously. Don't schedule back-to-back meetings in different cities
on the same day.

## 3. The dry season is fire season (May–September)

**82% canopy cover** on the parcel. **Dry season = May through September**.
Any outdoor work that involves fire (tatakuá builds, construction torches,
controlled burns) is **fire-risk** during those months.

- Have firebreaks (R01 plan pending)
- Water points + pump stations needed
- Outdoor burning banned during dry season (Municipalidad enforces)

**Practical impact:** Tatakuá restaurant only operates in wet season
(Nov–April) unless you build a fully-enclosed wood-fired oven with
spark arrestors.

## 4. Spanish vs Guaraní vs Jopara

Paraguay is officially **bilingual**: Spanish + Guaraní. Most rural
people speak Jopara (mixed). San Bernardino is mostly Spanish-speaking
but workers may switch.

- **Always have a Spanish speaker** for worker meetings (Sonja)
- **Don't assume English** — even at the airport, taxi drivers often
  speak only Spanish or Jopara
- **Guaraní numbers** are different from Spanish (1 = peteĩ, 2 = mokõi,
  3 = mbohapyhague — useful for bargaining at markets)

## 5. Time zones are weird

Paraguay is **UTC-4** (PYT). **No daylight saving time** since 2010.
That means:

- **PY = Amsterdam − 5 hours in summer** (PY 9am = NL 3pm)
- **PY = Amsterdam − 6 hours in winter** (PY 9am = NL 2pm)
- **Best call window:** 9am–12pm PY = 3pm–6pm NL summer / 2pm–5pm NL winter

Wes is in NL. Schedule calls within those windows. Late evening in NL =
midnight in PY, so don't expect prompt replies.

## 6. Legal structure takes longer than you think

The 4-BV cascade requires:
- **Notary** (Escribana) for each BV formation: ~2-4 weeks per BV
- **Tax registration** (SET, Paraguay tax authority): 1-2 weeks
- **NL BV formation** via KVK (Kamer van Koophandel): 1-2 weeks
- **Bank account** opening: 2-4 weeks per BV (especially for PY banks)

**Realistic timeline: 3-4 months** to get all 4 BVs operational from
zero. That's why W0.1 (attorney call) is the first priority.

## 7. Insurance in Paraguay is thin

The international insurers (Marsh, Aon) have limited PY presence.
Most property insurance is through **Mapfre Paraguay** or local brokers.
Liability coverage is harder to get than property coverage.

**Workaround:** Consider a **captive insurer** in Uruguay or
**fronting policy** through a NL insurer with PY reinsurance.
This is one of the reasons W0.7 (insurance broker pre-qualification)
is a hard gate.

## 8. Workers + payroll is different from NL

- **Aguinaldo** (13th month salary, paid in June + December): mandatory
- **IPS** (social security): ~9% employer + 9% employee contributions
- **Vacation**: 30 working days per year minimum after 1 year
- **Indemnization**: 1 month per year of service on unjustified dismissal
- **Notice period**: 30 days minimum
- **Workday**: 8 hours (max 48/week, no more than 4 hours overtime)

Sonja handles all of this — **don't try to DIY HR in PY**.

## 9. Construction takes longer than you think

- **Cob drying time** = 2-4 weeks per course (you can't stack fast)
- **Bamboo treatment** = 2 weeks (borate soak) + 1 week drying
- **Concrete cure** = 28 days for full strength
- **Permit approval** = 2-6 months (Municipalidad + MADES + INAA)

Plan a **3-year build**, not a 1-year build. Phase 1 (5 cabins + restaurant
+ pool) is 6-9 months of construction alone.

## 10. Internet + cell coverage is intermittent

Tigo + Personal + Claro all cover the area, but signal drops in
valleys. Starlink is the **reliable workaround** (R47 researched):

- **Starlink kit:** ~$600 USD + $80/month
- **Multi-dish setup** for 30-cabin park: ~$10K upfront + $1500/month
- Latency: 30-50ms (good for video calls, acceptable for everything)

## 14. Things can fail

This is a real project with real risks:

- **Fire** (R01 plan mitigates but doesn't eliminate)
- **Storm** (Q4 2026-Q1 2027 rainy season = construction delays)
- **Legal** (4-BV setup could hit tax authority questions)
- **Financial** (currency, inflation, IB threshold changes)
- **Team** (Sonja's availability, Kiki's network strength, Wes's time)
- **Construction** (worker availability, material delays, weather)

The repo is honest about these. R-series in
[`docs/RESEARCH_GAPS.md`](../research/strategy/RESEARCH_GAPS.md) is the risk register.
