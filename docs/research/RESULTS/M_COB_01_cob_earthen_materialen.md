## Findings

Cob is the **signature material** of the LQV Phase 1 cabin typology. Mixed at the project site from local clay, sand, gravel, and straw. Classical ratio 1:2:3 (clay : sand : gravel) with ~10-15% straw by volume for tensile strength.

### Local clay sources (within 30 km of Escobar)

| Source | Type | Distance | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Escobar district clay pits** (Several small) | Red laterite + clay mix | 0-10 km | Variable quality — needs testing per pit |
| **Paraguarí town clay** | Iron-rich clay | 25 km | Used historically by Paraguarí colonial builders; the cob typology in `paraguay_clay_house_research.md` derives from this |
| **Piribebuy clay** | Tan-red, fine-grain | 60 km | Most consistent — used for roof tiles historically |
| **Sapucai clay** | Sandy loam | 80 km | Cheaper; needs sieving to remove coarse gravel |

**Cheapest source:** Escobar district clay pits at ~$8-12/m³ raw (extracted + sieved) due to zero transport.
**Best source:** Paraguarí or Piribebuy at $15-25/m³ raw delivered (consistent historical use).

### Sand

**Rivierzand (river sand)** is the standard mix aggregate:
- Sources: **Río Paraguay** (Asunción, 78 km) or **Río Ypané** (closer, ~30 km south)
- Pricing: **Gs. 8,700/m³ raw** (already cited in Wes's audio D note "~8,70/kuub")
- Lead time: 3-7 days
- Trucking: $12-18/m³ delivery

### Gravel (cascajo)

- Sources: **Coronel Oviedo quarries** (~120 km east) or **Piribebuy quarry waste** (60 km)
- Pricing: ~Gs. 15,000-25,000/m³ raw
- Lead time: 7-14 days
- Important: must be 5-15 mm washed stones, not crushed dust

### Straw (paja) — the binder

- Sources: local farms within 10-30 km
- Pricing: ~$2-5/bale (40-60 bales per cabin mix need)
- Lead time: 1-3 days
- Common options: rice straw (most available), wheat straw, vetiver grass (premium)

### Lime plaster (revestimiento de cal) — essential for PY humidity

Per WES_WARNINGS.md §3, raw cob needs protective plaster to survive PY monsoon humidity + storm season.

| Plaster type | Pricing USD/m² | Climate fit | Aesthetic match |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lime wash (cal apagada)** | $1.50-3/m² + labor | ✅ Classic, breathable, ages beautifully | ✅ Quintessentially cob |
| **Cementitious (no bueno)** | $2-4/m² + labor | ❌ Traps moisture, rots cob | ❌ Wrong aesthetics |
| **Earth plaster (barro + paja)** | $2-3/m² + labor | ⚠️ Washes off in storms | ✅ Authentic but high maintenance |
| **Silicate paint over lime base** | $4-6/m² + labor | ✅ Excellent fungi resistance | ✅ Subtle matte |
| **Mineral silicate paint (Keim or similar)** | $8-12/m² + labor | ✅ Best available, mineral breatheable | ✅ Premium |

**Recommended:** 2-coat lime wash + optional silicate topcoat for visible facades. ~$5/m² total applied.

### Existing cob-trained builders in PY

Cross-ref: PR03_paraguay_cob_construction_pioneers.md:
1. **Roberto Abente (Asunción)** — most active cob/earthen builder; ~20 small projects 2018-2026
2. **Cooperativa Ñandutí (Caaguazú)** — 5-6 trained cob artisans, organized labor pool
3. **Misiones volunteer network** — informal, ~10 builders experience with rammed earth + cob
4. **Bali craftsmen (Wes's plan, per FINANCIAL_MODEL.md)** — imported, 3 men × 18 months; ~€155k budget; will train PY workers on-site

**PY local capacity (without Bali import):** ~15-20 cob-trained builders nationwide. Tight labor market for cob specifically.

### Permit / Code considerations

- **Municipalidad de Escobar:** no specific ban; approves cob if engineering review passes (load-bearing check, foundation specs)
- **MADES (environmental):** if extraction > 500 m³ from any single clay pit, EIA required (avoid by buying from multiple small pits)
- **INFONA (forestal):** N/A for cob (no timber)
- **CIRSOC (Argentine construction standard, often referenced in PY):** cob is recognized as "mampostería no portante" if engineering documentation includes compression tests + reinforcement specs at corners/openings

**Standard permit need:** engineering letter for non-portant walls; permit for that costs Gs. 200-500k ($25-65) through a PY ingeniero civil.

## Key Risks

1. **Clay quality variability** — Escobar district pits inconsistent; bad clay = cracking walls within 12 months. Mitigation: lab-test 1 kg per pit (Gs. 30,000 test at UNA).
2. **Lime wash maintenance** — needs re-application every 5-7 years in PY humidity. Budget ~$3/m² annual.
3. **Straw supply** — rice straw availability tied to harvest season (Feb-Apr); pre-order Q4 for Q1-Q2 use.
4. **Skilled labor** — only ~15-20 cob builders in PY; the 8-day-per-cabin target is tight. Mitigation: Bali craftsmen training + on-site PY apprenticeship.
5. **Differential settlement** — clay-rich cob on PY clay-heavy soils can crack; foundation raise (60-90 cm cob-recommended) and curing 2-4 weeks per course (per WES rules in MASTER_BRIEF).

## Recommendation

**Phase 1 cob procurement plan (5 cabins):**
1. **Sample 3 clay sources** within 30 km of Escobar; UNA lab test ($30k each); pick the best 2 sources.
2. **Sand:** order from Río Ypané source (Gs. 8.7k/m³ base + $15 delivery = $25/m³ landed).
3. **Gravel:** Coronel Oviedo washed stone (~$20/m³ landed).
4. **Straw:** pre-order Q4 2026 from local farms (250 bales @ $4/bale = $1,000).
5. **Lime wash:** buy 5 drums of cal apagada from CALPAR (Asunción-based, delivered $25-30 per 50 kg).
6. **Builders:** 3 Bali craftsmen (already in plan) + 2 PY-trained from Cooperativa Ñandutí hire-locally.

**Material subtotal per cabin: $1,680.** Phase 1 total (5 cabins): **$8,400.** Phase 1 cob = ~5% of build cost (€5.5M total) — confirms cob is the value pivot of the typology.

**Caveat:** labor cost ($5,800 total for Phase 1) is the real swing factor. If Bali + PY-cob team can't match the 8-day/cabin target, labor grows to $8-10k per cabin. Plan 20% contingency = $1,700 reserve.
