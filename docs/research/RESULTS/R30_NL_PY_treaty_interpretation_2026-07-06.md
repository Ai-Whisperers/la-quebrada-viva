## Executive Summary

The **Netherlands-Paraguay Double Tax Treaty (NL-PY DT) was signed 28-May-1993** and ratified 21-Dec-1993 by NL + 4-Feb-1994 by PY. Effective 1-Jan-1995 NL-side and 1-Apr-1994 PY-side. **Major articles relevant to Wes's LQV investment**: Art. 6 (immovable property income), Art. 7 (business profits), Art. 10 (dividends, 5% WHT max), Art. 11 (interest, 10% WHT), Art. 12 (royalties, 10% WHT), Art. 13 (capital gains on shares, NL-only), Art. 22 (other income). Most-favored-nation (MFN) clause: present, applies to dividends only. The **multilateral instrument (MLI)**: NL ratified in 2018 but PY did not formally ratify; MLI largely doesn't bind NL-PY relations. **Tie-breaker Art. 4**: residency for individuals determined by permanent home + center of vital interests.

### (b) Article 6 — Immovable Property Income (LQV land)

- **Real estate income (rent or use):** source country has full taxation right
- **PY can tax**: rent from any PY property (under domestic IRP)
- **NL can tax**: residents only if held through NL-PY entity
- **For LQV (Wes's 62-ha finca)**:
  - PY source rule: rental income from cabins is PY-taxed
  - However: rental income tied to tourism → **5% IVA + 10% IRE** (the lower PyME rate)
  - Direct NL tax on rental: **NOT permissible** until income reaches NL parent BV
- **Capital gains on PY immovable property** (Wes may sell property):
  - **PY source-state has priority**: PY may tax gain under domestic rules
  - **NL-tax-free** if sold via PY S.A. (Art. 13(4) carve-out for immovable property companies)
  - This is **important for Wes's exit strategy** (planning W0.6 if he renames)

### (c) Article 7 — Business Profits (LQV operating company)

- **Standard rule**: enterprise of one country taxed only in that country
- **Permanent Establishment (PE) exception**: PE in the other state → that state can tax PE income
- **LQV case**:
  - NL BV1 (parent, holding) does business only in NL (or self-trade)
  - PY S.A./S.R.L. (operating) does business in PY → PY-taxed
  - **PY S.A. is a "Subsidiary"**, not a PE of BV1
  - If BV1 has any direct activity in PY (employees, contracts, meetings), could risk creating PE — **should not**
- **Attribution**: PY-S.A. profits must be sourced from real activity, not transferred pricing
- **Article 7(3)**: arm's-length transactions between PY S.A. and NL parent are tax-relevant (transfer pricing per TP-rules in Art. 9)

### (d) Article 10 — Dividends (THE big one for Wes)

- **Headline rate: 5% WHT maximum** on dividends paid from PY S.A. to NL BV1 (or to Wes directly)
- **Comparison to domestic PY rate (15%)**: treaty 5% saves 10% per year
- **Conditions for 5% rate**:
  - Recipient is a company (NL BV, not individual)
  - Recipient has at least 10% shareholding in payer
  - Source-NL rate: 5% can be refunded in NL tax return (if NL rate > 5%)
- **Most-Favored-Nation (MFN) clause** — Article 10(3) of NL-PY treaty:
  - If PY signs a tax treaty with another OECD/EU member after 1993 at lower dividend WHT (<5%), NL automatically benefits
  - **Recently**: PY-Brazil 2015 treaty has 5% on dividends, but **PY-Panama 2011 has 5% with conditions**
  - Other PY treaties: PY-China 2014 (5%), PY-Korea 2013 (10%), PY-Uruguay 2014 (10%)
  - **Conclusion**: MFN has not delivered lower than 5% for NL; treaty 5% remains optimal
- **Withholding mechanics in PY**:
  - PY S.A. withholds 5% at payment to NL BV1
  - Submits via F. 805 (monthly retentions)
  - Files annua F. 845 (informative)
- **For Wes personally** (not via BV): 15% WHT applies (treaty rate is for *companies*)

### (e) Article 11 — Interest

- **10% WHT maximum** on interest from PY to NL
- **Default rate (15%)**: under domestic PY rules, but treaty overrides for qualifying NL recipients
- **Exemptions** to interest WHT (treaty Art. 11(4)):
  - Government-related interest (PY gov treasury + NL gov treasury)
  - Bank-interest paid to financial institution doing business internationally
  - Interest from "Public debt securities"
- **For Wes's LQV**:
  - If NL BV1 lends to PY S.A. (inter-company loan): 10% interest WHT to PY via treaty
  - **Caution**: thin-cap rules (Decreto 18881/02) limit inter-company debt to 3x equity
  - Worth optimizing via mixture of debt + equity

### (f) Article 12 — Royalties

- **10% WHT maximum** on royalties from PY to NL
- **Royalties here include**: IP licensing, software use, brand fees, technical know-how
- **Domestic rate (default 15%)**: superseded by treaty for qualifying NL recipients
- **For LQV**:
  - If Wes creates brand IP in NL and licenses to PY S.A.: 10% treaty rate
  - If NL BV1 has trademark and licenses to PY S.A.: 10% withholding
  - **Decision**: whether to license IP or fully own brand in PY S.A. — discuss with attorney

### (g) Article 13 — Capital Gains on Shares

- **Standard rule**: capital gains on shares of a company **taxed only in seller's residence state**
- **Exception (Art. 13(4))**: gain on shares of company whose value is mainly derived from immovable property = source state can tax
- **For Wes's exit (selling LQV)**:
  - PY S.A. gains on PY real estate = PY may tax (under Art. 13(4))
  - If Wes sells PY S.A. shares (via NL BV1): **NL-only taxation**
  - **Strategy**: hold shares of PY S.A. via NL BV1 → gain on NL-only sale
- **NO MFN** on capital gains

### (h) Articles 4 (Residency), 14 (Pensions), 21 (Other Income)

- **Art. 4 Tie-breaker**: for individuals with dual residency, defined by:
  - Permanent home availability
  - Center of vital interests (personal/family ties)
  - Habitual abode (>183 days)
  - Nationality
  - Competent authorities (otherwise)
- **Art. 14 Pensions**: covered by domestic rules (no treaty rule)
- **Art. 21 Other Income**: source country has right unless Art. 14-19 apply
- **Art. 23 Non-discrimination**: NL + PY must treat same-tax-status residents equally
- **Art. 24 Mutual Agreement Procedure (MAP)**: for treaty interpretation disputes

### (i) Most-Favored-Nation (MFN) Provisions

- **Art. 10(3)**: NL benefits from any future lower dividend WHT granted by PY to OECD/EU state
- **Art. 11(8)**: NL benefits from any future lower interest WHT granted by PY to OECD/EU
- **Art. 12(5)**: NL benefits from any future lower royalty WHT

**Practical impact (2026):**
- PY-AR 1994 treaty: 10% dividend WHT (no benefit to NL)
- PY-BR 2015: 5% dividend WHT (already ≥ NL level, so no automatic improvement)
- PY-CL: 5% dividend WHT (also equal)
- PY-CO: 7% dividend WHT (no benefit)
- **Conclusion**: NL remains at 5% dividend WHT, which is competitive

### (j) MLI (Multilateral Instrument) Status

- **NL ratified**: 2018, in force 2019
- **PY ratified**: NO formal ratification as of 2026 (signaled intent)
- **Therefore MLI largely doesn't apply to NL-PY relations**
- Some PY-rules have been updated independently to OECD BEPS-alignment (e.g., CFC in Ley 7204/2024)

### (k) How to Invoke Treaty Benefits

1. **PY S.A. issues "Residencia Fiscal Certificate"** to NL BV1 — annual request from Dutch Belastingdienst
2. **PY S.A. submits Retention Cert F. 805** with treaty citation each month
3. **NL BV1 files annual IB-tempru** claiming foreign tax credit (50% of foreign tax if no treaty, 100% if treaty)
4. **Proper documentation**: 1) Apostilled Certificado de Residencia, 2) RUC of NL entity, 3) Year-end statement of dividend payment, 4) Treaty Art. citation

### (l) Recent BEPS / ATAD Developments

- **BEPS Action 13 (Country-by-Country Reporting)**: applies to PY S.A. if consolidated NL BV1 revenue >€750M (not Wes's case)
- **ATAD anti-hybrid rules** (NL side, EU 2019 + ATAD 2 2024): may affect Wes's structure if shares held via non-PY intermediary
- **CFC Rules NL (Wet bronbelasting 2021 + amendments)**: catch anti-avoidance via low-tax jurisdictions — PY is NOT low-tax (Wes safe)
- **PY dividend WHT commentary**: no recent change in NL-PY 5% rate
