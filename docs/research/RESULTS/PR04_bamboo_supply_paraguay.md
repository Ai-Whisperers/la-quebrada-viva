## Summary

Paraguay has **native Guadua angustifolia** (tacuara guazú) bamboo, one of the strongest bamboo species in the world (compressive strength 8,000+ PSI — comparable to steel). Guadua is the **same species used in Colombia/Ecuador** for seismic-resistant construction. It's naturally abundant in PY, especially in the eastern departments (Alto Paraná, Caaguazú, Paraguarí — yes, including our region).

**For RV:** Bamboo is the **second-pillar material** (after cob) for accent + structural elements. PY has both natural supply + a tradition of bamboo craftsmanship (used in colonial-era buildings).

## Key Data Points

- **Guadua angustifolia** (tacuara guazú) — native to PY, strongest bamboo species
- **Growth rate:** Guadua can grow **30-100 cm/day** in optimal conditions; full culm maturity in 3-5 years
- **PY supply:** Wild + plantation; Paraguarí department has natural stands
- **Compressive strength:** 8,000+ PSI (similar to steel reinforcement in concrete)
- **Traditional uses in PY:** Colonial roof structures, fencing, fishing equipment, basket-weaving
- **Modern uses:** Bridges, scaffolding, flooring, structural framing (post-tensioned)
- **Cost estimate (from repo's c02 doc):** ~$1-3 per linear meter for raw culms, $5-15 per m² for finished bamboo flooring
- **Sustainability:** Guadua's fast growth + CO2 sequestration make it the most carbon-negative building material
- **Risk:** Bamboo beetle (Dinoderus minutus) can destroy untreated bamboo — requires treatment
