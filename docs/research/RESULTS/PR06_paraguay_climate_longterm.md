## Summary

Paraguay has a **humid subtropical climate** in the eastern region (where RV is). **Average annual temperature:** 23°C. **Average annual rainfall:** 1,500-1,800 mm in Paraguarí. **Distinct wet/dry seasons:** October-April (hot, wet, summer) and May-September (cool, dry, winter). **Climate change is intensifying both:** summers hotter + wetter, winters drier, more frequent extreme events (droughts, floods, severe storms).

**For RV:** The parcel is in the **moderate-climate zone** (Paraguarí averages 22-24°C year-round). Climate change is the **biggest long-term risk** to the project — droughts affect water supply + forest health; floods affect access road + quebrada cabin placement; heat waves affect guest comfort.

## Key Data Points

- **Climate classification:** Humid subtropical (Cfa in Köppen)
- **Annual rainfall:** 1,500-1,800 mm (Paraguarí dept)
- **Rainy season:** Oct-Apr (70% of annual rainfall)
- **Dry season:** May-Sep (5-30 mm/month)
- **Temperature range:** 5°C (winter lows) to 40°C (summer highs)
- **Mean annual temp:** 22-23°C
- **Extreme events (2008-2024):** Major floods in 2014, 2015, 2019; major droughts in 2008, 2011, 2022
- **Climate change trend:** +0.5-1°C warming since 1980; 10-15% increase in extreme rainfall events
- **2024-2025:** El Niño brought drought to PY in late 2024; La Niña forecast for 2025-2026

## Implications for the Project

- **Water supply:** Need year-round water source. Quebrada is seasonal — dry season flow can be 10% of wet season. Need cistern + backup well (per F15 cistern sizing)

- **Insurance:** Climate change is driving insurance premium increases worldwide — factor into L22 insurance minimums
- **Guest experience:** "Shoulder season" (Apr-May, Sep-Oct) offers best weather — could become prime booking season
- **Long-term planning:** Plan for hotter, drier, more extreme climate by 2030-2050; prioritize drought-resistant native species in reforestation (c08 native plants list)
