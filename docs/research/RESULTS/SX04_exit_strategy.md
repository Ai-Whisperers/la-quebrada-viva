# SX04 — Exit strategy options for LQV

**Date:** 2026-06-30
**Status:** MEM-based. Long-term strategic planning.

## What SX04 is

**LQV's 5-year+ exit options for Wes + Thijs + investors.**

## Exit options ranked

### Option 1: Sale to international hotel chain (most likely)
**Year:** 5-7 years (Y7-9 of operation)
**Buyer profile:** Eco-lodge chain (Awasi, Aman, Six Senses, 1 Hotels)
**Valuation:** 5-8x annual revenue (typical hospitality multiple)
**For LQV Y5-7:** $5-12M sale price (per scenarios 3a-3c)
**Net to Wes + Thijs (75% equity):** $3.75-9M
**Net to NL investors (25% via BV2):** $1.25-3M

**Process:**
- Engage boutique hotel broker (e.g. HVS, Christie & Co)
- Target eco-lodges
- NDAs + site visits
- 6-12 month sales process

**Pros:** Best ROI, clean exit, "success" validates the project
**Cons:** Loses operational control, may not match Wes's lifestyle goals

### Option 2: Sale to long-stay operator (alternative path)
**Year:** 5-8 years
**Buyer profile:** Long-stay operator, coliving network
**Valuation:** 4-6x annual revenue
**For LQV:** $4-8M
**Net to Wes + Thijs:** $3-6M
**Net to NL investors:** $1-2M

**Pros:** Maintains eco-credibility if buyer shares values
**Cons:** Smaller market, less capital

### Option 3: Family succession (Wes's kids)
**Year:** 10-15+ years
**Mechanism:** Wes + Thijs retain ownership, hand operations to family
**Valuation:** N/A (no exit event, ongoing income)
**Net to Wes + Thijs:** Ongoing revenue stream
**Net to investors:** Return of capital + ongoing dividend

**Pros:** Maintains project vision, lifestyle alignment
**Cons:** Long-term commitment, family health risk

### Option 4: Investor share buy-back
**Year:** 7-10 years
**Mechanism:** BV2 (NL) buys back investor shares at pre-agreed multiple (e.g. 2-3x)
**Valuation:** Pre-agreed formula
**For LQV:** $1-3M to buy back 25% of equity (if all investors want out)
**Net to investors:** Full exit with profit

**Pros:** Clean exit for investors, Wes + Thijs retain 100% control
**Cons:** Large capital outflow, may need bridge financing

### Option 5: ESOP (employee stock ownership plan)
**Year:** 7-10 years
**Mechanism:** Transfer 10-30% of equity to key staff (per L32)
**Valuation:** N/A (not an exit event, internal restructuring)
**Net to Wes + Thijs:** Partial liquidity (the 70-90% they keep)
**Net to investors:** Their equity percentage slightly diluted

**Pros:** Aligns staff with long-term success, retains talent
**Cons:** Wes + Thijs don't fully exit, just partially

### Option 6: REIT/Trust structure
**Year:** 5-8 years
**Mechanism:** Transfer ownership to a trust or REIT, sell shares to public
**Valuation:** Real estate multiple
**For LQV:** $5-10M
**Net to Wes + Thijs:** Depends on structure

**Pros:** Liquidity, professional management
**Cons:** Complex, regulatory burden, loss of control

### Option 7: Continue operating indefinitely
**Year:** Ongoing
**Mechanism:** Wes + Thijs + investors keep ownership, run for life
**Valuation:** Annual profit + asset value
**For Wes + Thijs:** Lifestyle + annual income ($200-500K)
**Net:** Stability over liquidity

**Pros:** Maintains vision + control
**Cons:** Limited exit, family risk (health, etc.)

## Recommended path for LQV

**Year 1-3:** Build to operational track record
- Phase 1 complete (5 cabins)
- Phase 2 started (10 more)
- 2-3 years of profitability
- Strong reviews + brand

**Year 3-5:** Decide on exit direction
- If profitability + reviews strong: Option 1 (hotel chain sale) is the path
- If profitability moderate: continue operating
- If life priorities: stay in family control

**Year 5-7:** First exit opportunity
- Per the 5-7 year track record
- Choose based on Wes's personal situation at that time

**Year 7-10:** Second exit or restructure
- If 5-7 year exit didn't happen: Option 5 (ESOP) + Option 4 (buy-back)
- Maintain long-term value for stakeholders
