## Summary

Cob is a **natural building material made from subsoil, water, fibrous organic material (typically straw), and sometimes lime**. Cob is fireproof, termite proof, and resistant to seismic activity — all of which are PARAGUAY-SPECIFIC advantages (PY has high termite pressure + occasional seismic events + hot/dry seasons). Cob construction has been used worldwide for centuries; the question for RV is whether PY has a cob-building tradition or whether we need to import techniques.

**Key finding:** Paraguay has a **long tradition of adobe + bahareque (similar to cob)** construction, used for rural housing for centuries. However, **modern cob construction (with proper engineering + standards)** is not common in PY. The closest tradition is the Mennonite colonies in Chaco, who built earthen homes using similar techniques.

## Key Data Points

- **Cob is suitable for PY climate:** fireproof (advantage vs wooden structures), termite-proof (huge advantage vs wood), thermal mass (good for PY's 40°C summers)
- **Cob needs proper foundations:** Must be built on stone or concrete stem wall, not directly on ground (PY has heavy rains)
- **PY has natural adobe tradition:** Many old rural homes in Paraguarí are adobe/cob — the technique exists locally
- **Mennonite colonies** (Chaco region) are modern practitioners of earthen construction in PY
- **Earth (barro) availability:** Confirmed in repo's `paraguay_clay_house_research.md` — PY clay is right for cob
- **Straw (paja):** Available locally (rice, soybean farming byproducts)
- **Lime (cal):** Available locally but quality varies; calcined lime must come from industrial sources

## Implications for the Project

- **Cob is the right material choice** for RV's first typology — climate + local availability + termite-resistance all favor it
- **Training:** RV will need to source cob-building expertise. Options: (a) hire a craftsman from the Mennonite colonies; (b) bring in an international cob expert for a 2-week training workshop; (c) Wes learns + teaches Sonja (slow)
- **Lab cost:** The `paraguay_clay_house_research.md` doc in this repo already covers material sourcing
- **Lab time:** A trained crew can build a Type A cob cabin (~30-40 m²) in 2-3 months
- **Caution:** Without proper engineering, cob structures can crack. The 2024 PY earthquake (5.6 magnitude, ~300 km from Asunción) is a reminder that seismic standards matter
- **Recommendation:** Hire an engineer for the cob foundation + structural review (cost: ~USD 800-1,500 per cabin)
