## Summary

In Paraguay, **water well drilling is regulated by MADES** (since the ENAPR/INAA restructuring in 2018). All wells deeper than 30m or with extraction >5,000 L/day require a **Concesión de Uso de Aguas Públicas** (Public Water Use Concession). For RV's Phase 1, expected water need is **~15,000-25,000 L/day** (5 cabins × 5 people × 250 L + restaurant + pool + irrigation). **MADS will require a full environmental permit application** (covered in PR07).

**Key finding:** The quebrada is a **surface water source** (not groundwater), which falls under different regulations (MADES Recursos Hídricos). RV likely needs **both** a well permit (for backup water) + a surface water permit (for quebrada irrigation/use).

## Key Data Points

- **Regulatory authority:** MADES - Dirección General de Protección y Conservación de los Recursos Hídricos
- **Required permits:**
  1. **Pozo (well):** Required for >5,000 L/day extraction
  2. **Aguas superficiales (surface water):** Required for any significant extraction from quebrada/río
  3. **Vertimiento (discharge):** Required if discharging wastewater (related to septic permit)
- **Process for well concession:**
  - Submit technical study (hydrogeologist report)
  - Specify: depth, expected yield, water quality test
  - Application fee: USD 200-500
  - Processing time: 60-180 days
  - Annual fee: USD 50-200 (depending on volume)
- **Process for surface water permit:**
  - Environmental impact assessment
  - Specify: intake location, daily volume, water use (irrigation, recreation)
  - Cost: USD 500-2,000 for EIA
  - Processing time: 90-180 days
- **PY water quality:** Generally good, but iron content can be high in some regions (treatment needed)
- **No central water utility in Escobar** — RV is fully self-sufficient for water (well + cistern + quebrada capture)
- **Cistern sizing (per F15):** ~30,000-50,000 L reserve needed for dry season
