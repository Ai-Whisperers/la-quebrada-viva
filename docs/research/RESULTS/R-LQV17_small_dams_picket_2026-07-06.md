## Anatomy of a picket dam (the classic quick-release dam)

A picket dam is a row of **vertical posts (pickets)** set into a concrete base across the stream, with **horizontal boards (stop-logs or needles)** stacked between them to hold back water. To open: lift the boards out (one or two at a time, top-down). To close: drop them back in.

**It's literally how millers controlled streams for 800 years in Europe and how the Dutch still manage their boezem water levels today.**

### Components
1. **Base (foundation):** reinforced concrete slab across the stream bed, 30 cm thick × 50 cm wider than the dam on each side, with the post sockets cast in.
2. **Pickets:** vertical posts (typically metal I-beams or treated hardwood), 1.0-1.5 m above the base, spaced 50-70 cm apart. Set into sockets so they can be removed if needed.
3. **Stop-logs (the boards):** horizontal planks (50×200 mm hardwood or aluminum), stacked between the pickets. Each board adds ~10-15 cm of water height.
4. **Sealing:** a rubber gasket or foam strip on the upstream face of each picket so water doesn't leak through the gaps.
5. **Sill (downstream apron):** concrete or stone, 1-1.5 m long, to prevent the falling water from scouring a hole in the stream bed.

### Why this is perfect for LQV
- **Open/close in 30-60 seconds per board** — no tools, no machinery, one person.
- **Adjustable water level** — add or remove boards to control pond depth.
- **Seasonal flexibility** — full pool in dry season (Jun-Sep), open in flood season (Oct-Mar) to let high water pass.
- **No moving metal parts to rust** — boards stack out of water when removed.
- **Visual:** a row of vertical posts with stacked timber boards looks **industrial-archaeological**, very on-brand for an Italianate eco-retreat. Think old mill race.
- **Repairable with hand tools** — if a board warps, replace it. If a post rots, dig it out and reset.

## Alternative dam types (when picket isn't right)

### Option B — Sandbag stop-log (cheaper, less elegant)
Stack sandbags on a low concrete sill (~30 cm above stream bed). To open: pull sandbags off. To close: restack.
- **Pros:** $50-100 per dam, anyone can build it in a day.
- **Cons:** ugly, sandbags degrade in UV (replace yearly), not "Italian style."
- **Use case:** temporary during construction, or for the irrigation basin only.

### Option C — Inflatable rubber dam (modern, expensive)
A rubber bladder inflated with air or water to hold back water; deflate to open.
- **Pros:** zero visual impact when deflated, very fast open/close (5 min).
- **Cons:** $3,000-8,000 per dam (industrial product), UV degrades rubber in 8-12 years, puncture risk.
- **Use case:** if you want an "invisible" dam when not in use. Probably overkill for LQV.

### Option D — Hinged flap gate (mechanical)
A steel or hardwood plate hinged at the bottom, lifted by a hand-cranked screw or counterweight.
- **Pros:** 30-second open/close with the crank, very robust, Italianate aesthetic if done in steel + stone.
- **Cons:** $1,500-2,500 per dam, moving parts need maintenance, requires precision metalwork.
- **Use case:** if you want a "feature" dam near the restaurant or main cabin — visual + functional.

**Recommendation:** stick with **picket dams for all 3**, but consider **one hinged flap gate** as a signature feature near the main building (the white house in the photo) — it's a beautiful object when well-made.

## Pitfalls (real failures)

1. **Scour downstream of the dam.** Water falling over the boards digs a hole. Fix: **stone apron 1.5 m long**, plus a row of large boulders (riprap) at the downstream toe.
2. **Leaks between boards.** The biggest annoyance. Fix: **EPDM gasket on each picket face**, plus a **light coat of bentonite grease** on the stop-log edges (renewed yearly).
3. **Boards warp.** Cheap pine warps in 6 months. Fix: **use hardwood only** (yvyra pyta, eucalyptus treated, or imported pine that's been kiln-dried after pressure-treatment). Or aluminum extrusions for zero warp at $300-500 extra per dam.
4. **Pickets rot at the base.** Wood in constant wet/dry cycle fails in 3-5 years. Fix: **set pickets in concrete sockets** so the wet/dry zone is in concrete, not wood. Or use steel I-beams with sacrificial thickness.
5. **Flood debris jams the boards.** A branch wedges between pickets, water rises, finds a path around the dam, erodes the bank. Fix: **a coarse trash rack** (vertical bars 10 cm apart) upstream of the dam, removable for cleaning.
6. **No overflow.** If you forget and it rains hard, the pond overtops the dam and erodes the bank behind it. Fix: **a fixed overflow pipe** at maximum desired water level — water spills through the pipe before it spills over the boards.
