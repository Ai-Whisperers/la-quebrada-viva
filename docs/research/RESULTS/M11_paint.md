## PY climate stress on exterior paint

- **UV index:** extreme (8-12) year-round, especially Oct-Feb
- **Humidity:** 70-85% in summer
- **Rainfall:** 1,500-1,800mm/year, mostly in summer
- **Heat:** 35-42°C summer days
- **Mold/mildew risk:** high (constant humidity)
- **Insects:** termites can attack some wood finishes

## Best paint types for RV

### Option 1: Acrylic exterior (recommended)

**Pros:**
- UV-resistant
- Breathable (allows moisture to escape)
- Good color retention
- Easy to apply
- Available everywhere in PY
- Inexpensive

**Cons:**
- May chalk after 3-5 years in extreme UV
- Needs primer for masonry
- Multiple coats for durability

**Brands available in PY:**
- Sherwin Williams (US brand, PY distribution)
- Pinturas Inca (PY, high quality)
- Pinturas Albalux (PY)
- Colorín (AR, imported)
- Sherwin Williams (premium)
- Duralba (AR)
- International brands at Sherwin Williams stores

**Cost for RV Fase 1:**
- Material: Gs. 35,000-60,000/gallon (3.5L)
- 1 gallon covers 30-40 m² (1 coat)
- Application labor: Gs. 15,000-25,000/m²
- Per-cabin exterior: ~$300-600
- **Fase 1 (5 cabins + reception):** ~$2,500-4,000

### Option 2: Elastomeric (best for masonry/concrete)

**Pros:**
- Bridges small cracks (typical for cob construction!)
- Waterproof
- UV-resistant
- 10+ year lifespan

**Cons:**
- Expensive
- Specialist application
- Limited color range
- Overkill for some surfaces

**Best for:** Cob walls, exterior stucco, concrete

**Brands:** Sherwin Williams, Sika, Mapei (imported)

**Cost:** 2-3x acrylic. ~$800-1,500 per cabin exterior.

### Option 3: Mineral paint (lime-based, traditional)

**Pros:**
- Authentic (matches RV's cob/earthen aesthetic)
- Breathable
- Naturally anti-mold (high pH)
- Low cost
- DIY-friendly
- Matches MASTER_BRIEF rule #2 (no cement plaster, use lime)

**Cons:**
- Less UV-resistant than acrylic (fades in 3-5 years)
- Limited color range (whites, earth tones)
- More frequent re-application

**Brands in PY:** Local fabricators, Centro Cultural (workshops)

**Cost:** ⅓ acrylic. ~$150-300 per cabin exterior.

**For RV (Wes's cob construction + "no cement plaster" rule):** Mineral/lime paint is the **most authentic choice**. Pairs with the lime mortar in the cob walls.

### Option 4: Wood stain (for the wood-cladding areas)

**Pros:**
- Shows wood grain
- Breathable
- Traditional look
- Long-lasting (5-10 years)

**Cons:**
- More maintenance than paint
- Color fades
- Needs re-staining

**For:** The wood-cladding portions of the cabins (eaves, beams, deck)

**Brands:** Cetol, Sikkens, Cuprinol (imported), local PY stains

**Cost:** ~$100-200 per cabin for stain application.

## Phasing

- **Fase 1:** Mineral paint on cob + wood stain on wood + acrylic in wet areas. Authentically RV.
- **Fase 2-3:** Same system, with maintenance plan (re-stain every 5 years, re-paint every 7-10 years)

## Anti-fungal considerations

PY's tropical climate = constant mold/mildew risk. **Use paint with mildewcide** built in (most exterior paints in PY have it). Additional options:
- **Anti-mold additive:** Add to paint at mixing time, ~Gs. 50,000 per cabin
- **Mildewcide wash:** Annual pre-monsoon treatment
- **Zinc strips on roof edge:** Prevent mold streaking on walls
- **Gutter system:** Divert rainwater away from walls
