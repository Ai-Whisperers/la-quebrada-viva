## Wine

**PY has a small but growing wine industry (mostly in the Itapúa / Alto Paraná region):**

| Producer | Style | Location | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bodegas de la Asunción** | Traditional + modern | Capiatá, Central | Multiple varietals |
| **Viñas de Altair** | Boutique | Itapúa | Small batch, limited distribution |
| **La Violeta** | Traditional | Paraguarí | Family operation |
| **Bodega de Ciervo Cua** | Modern | Caaguazú | Award-winning |

**Wine quality in PY:**
- Improved dramatically in last 10 years
- Mostly red varietals: Tannat (best), Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot
- Whites: Sauvignon Blanc, Viognier
- **Challenge:** Hot climate = high alcohol, low acidity
- **Price:** Gs. 30,000-80,000/bottle for quality PY wine (similar to mid-range AR or CL)

**For RV restaurant (Fase 1):**
- Stock 3-4 PY wines (red, white, rosé)
- Pair with European wines for the "international guest" option
- Build a wine list that tells the PY story + has international coverage

**RV's wine budget (Fase 1):**
- Initial stock: 100-200 bottles
- Replacement: 20-30 bottles/month
- Cost: ~Gs. 1,000,000-1,500,000/month for wine inventory

## Cheese

**PY has a small cheese industry (mostly Mennonite colonies, dairy farms):**

| Producer | Style | Location |
|---|---|---|
| **Cooperativa Chortitzer** | Mennonite-style cheese | Loma Plata |
| **Lacteos Blumenort** | Various, including queso Paraguay | Loma Plata |
| **Cooperativa Neuland** | Cheese + dairy | Neuland |
| **Loma Plata dairy farms** | Bulk cheese | Loma Plata region |
| **Queso Paraguay** (small artisanal) | Fresh, semi-aged | Various |

**PY cheese varieties:**
- **Queso Paraguay** (fresh, white, mild — like fresh mozzarella)
- **Queso Paraguay de hoja** (wrapped in leaf)
- **Queso Paraguay estacionado** (aged, harder)
- **Mennonite-style** (similar to European gouda/edam)
- **Queso criollo** (traditional)

**Quality:** Very good for fresh, decent for aged
**Price:** Gs. 25,000-60,000/kg (varies widely)

**For RV:**
- Feature queso Paraguay on the menu (especially as appetizer or dessert)
- Source from Mennonite colonies (Loma Plata)
- Build a "cheese plate" with local varieties + European cheeses
- Cost: Gs. 500,000-1,000,000/month for cheese inventory

## Chocolate

**PY has a small artisanal chocolate scene:**

| Producer | Style | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cacao de los Jesuitas** | Bean-to-bar, traditional | Based on Jesuit missions heritage |
| **Maria's Cacao** | Modern bean-to-bar | Asunción-based, growing brand |
| **Carmelo Cacao** | Artisanal | Small batch |
| **ChocoSur** | Single-origin | Specialty focus |

**PY has historical cacao heritage (Jesuit missions 17th-18th century) but is NOT a major cacao producer today.** The industry is being revived by small entrepreneurs.

**Quality:** Excellent (small batch, careful fermentation)
**Price:** Gs. 50,000-150,000/bar (200g)

**For RV:**
- Source for the restaurant's dessert menu
- Marketing story ("recovered from Jesuit heritage")
- Cost: Gs. 300,000-500,000/month for chocolate inventory

## Coffee

**PY has a tiny coffee industry (not a major coffee producer, but some specialty):**

| Producer | Style | Location |
|---|---|---|
| **Cafés del Sur** | Specialty, small batch | Itapúa, Alto Paraná |
| **Cooperativa La Paz** | Multiple, small | Caaguazú |
| **Various individual farms** | Each ~5-10 ha | Eastern PY |

**PY's coffee status:**
- Not a major producer (climate too hot, no highland microclimates)
- Small specialty scene exists (for differentiation)
- Most coffee consumed in PY is Brazilian or Colombian
- **Recommendation:** Don't lead with PY coffee; offer it as a novelty in the restaurant

**Price:** Gs. 30,000-100,000/kg (rare, gift-quality)
