## Scenario 3a — Slow & steady growth (conservative)

**Assumptions:**
- Phase 1: 5 cabins (2026-2027)
- Phase 2: +10 cabins (2027-2029)
- Phase 3: +15 cabins (2029-2031)
- Total: 30 cabins by 2031 (slower than 2029 target)
- ADR growth: 3-5% per year
- Occupancy: 30% Year 1, 50% Year 2, 60% Y3+
- Investment: BV2 (NL) raises €3M over 3 years

**P&L (per year, by 2030):**
- Revenue: $400-500K
- Operating costs: $300-350K
- Net: $100-150K/year
- 10-year cumulative: $1.5-2M net

**Exit options:**
- Y8-10: sell to hotel chain at $5-7M
- Or: continue operating, $1-1.5M annual profit by 2030+

**Pros:** Low risk, gradual, manageable
**Cons:** Lower ROI, slower growth

## Scenario 3b — Aggressive growth (mid-case)

**Exit options:**

**Pros:** Matches Wes's plan, high growth, attractive exit

## Scenario 3c — Hyper-growth (with international capital)

**Assumptions:**
- Phase 1: 5 cabins (2026)
- Phase 2: +10 cabins (2027) + restaurant expansion

**Pros:** Best ROI, fastest growth
**Cons:** Highest risk, requires strong execution

## Scenario 3d — Pivot to long-stay (post-pandemic market)

**Assumptions:**
- Phase 1: 5 cabins (2026) + 2 long-stay apartments
- Phase 2: +10 long-stay apartments (2027-2028)
- Phase 3: +5 long-stay + 5 traditional cabins
- Total: 12 long-stay + 10 traditional = 22 units
- ADR: long-stay lower per day but longer stays
- Occupancy: long-stay 70-80% (longer stays = more reliable)

**P&L (per year, by 2030):**
- Revenue: $500-700K
- Operating costs: $300-400K (lower per unit, less turnover)
- Net: $200-300K/year
- 10-year cumulative: $3-4M net

**Exit options:**
- Y5-8: sell to long-stay operator at $6-8M
- Or: continue as boutique

**Pros:** Less seasonality, more stable revenue
**Cons:** Lower ADR, slower growth
