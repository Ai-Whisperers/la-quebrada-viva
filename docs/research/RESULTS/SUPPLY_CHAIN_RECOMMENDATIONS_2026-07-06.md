## D) Negotiation protocol (NEW01 playbook)

The 5-step vendor negotiation script (from `NEW01_ai_price_negotiator.md`):

### Step 1: Messaging first, never call
- Open every vendor relationship with a Messaging message
- Voice calls = high-pressure; Messaging = the vendor can take time to respond
- Send the requirement, the volume, the timeline, the budget
- Get a written quote in Messaging (the receipt of price)

### Step 2: Anchor high, walk away
- Quote 3 vendors, get 3 written quotes
- Tell each vendor the other 2 quotes (this is normal in PY business)
- The lowest quote is your anchor; reject it, ask for 10-15% lower
- Walk away from the negotiation (literally leave the conversation for 24 hr)
- The vendor will come back with a better price (NEW01 documents the $4,000 off car precedent)

### Step 3: Common PY vendor terms
- **30% upfront, 40% on delivery, 30% on completion** is the standard
- Negotiate: 20% upfront, 50% on delivery, 30% on completion
- Never accept: 50% upfront (cash-flow trap), no RUC (no factura = no tax compliance), "send me the account for transfer" (means vendor has no formal business)

### Step 4: Get a factura
- Always demand a factura (formal invoice) with RUC + timbrado
- This is the tax compliance chain for Wes's IRP/IRE obligations
- A vendor who refuses facturas is a red flag (probably informal economy, not paying taxes, no recourse if defective work)

### Step 5: The "I have other vendors" leverage
- After Step 2's walk-away, send a short follow-up: "We have other vendors also bidding this work. Your quote is X% above the lowest. Can you match?"
- The vendor will often match or split the difference
- The 5-10% saving compounds across all 16 categories (potential €15-25K saving on Phase 1)

**Common PY vendor traps to avoid**:
- "Cash price" without factura (15-20% cheaper, but no tax compliance)
- "I'll bring the materials myself" (vendor markup is 20-40% on materials)
- "Same as last time" (no written quote, no comparison baseline)
- "Give me the full amount upfront" (NEVER — this is the standard advance-fee scam)
- "I know a guy" (no formal vendor, no recourse)
