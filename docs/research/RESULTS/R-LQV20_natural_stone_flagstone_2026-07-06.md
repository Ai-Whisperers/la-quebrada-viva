## The 4 main natural stone types at LQV (PY Atlantic Forest region)

### 1. **Arenisca (Sandstone)** — Piribebuy, Cerro Vera, Ybycuí
- **Color range:** warm beige → ochre → terracotta red (depending on iron oxide content)
- **Texture:** medium-grained, can be split into flat flagstones OR cut into blocks
- **Best use:** Italian-style facing, bridge voussoirs (R-LQV16), terraced walls, garden paths, fireplace surrounds
- **Why it's like Dutch flagstone:** both are sedimentary, split into flat layers, warm earthy color. Dutch "flagstone" comes in similar beige-grey; PY arenisca comes in warmer beige-red.
- **Cost:** $10-18/m³ at quarry, $25-40/m² delivered and cut to 3-5 cm thickness
- **Suppliers:** see §Suppliers below

### 2. **Granito (Granite)** — Itá, Paraguarí, Quiindy
- **Color range:** grey-pink → charcoal → speckled black-and-white (depending on quarry)
- **Texture:** coarse-grained, takes a polish but works rough-cut too
- **Best use:** Italian Pietra Serena substitute for floors + counter-tops + exterior cladding, cobblestone paving
- **Why it's like Italian granite:** Itá granite is geologically similar to Sardinian and Tuscan granites — same family of intrusive igneous rock. Italianate cobblestone ("sampietrini" look) is achievable.
- **Cost:** $15-25/m³ at quarry, $35-60/m² delivered and cut to 2-3 cm thickness
- **Suppliers:** **Cantera Ita Porã** (Paraguarí, 25 km from LQV!) is the specialist

### 3. **Cuarcita (Quartzite)** — Caazapá, Misiones
- **Color range:** silver-grey → bluish-grey → golden-brown
- **Texture:** very hard, splits into flat flagstones naturally, sparkles slightly in sunlight
- **Best use:** the closest PY equivalent to Dutch/German "leisteen" (slate) and Brazilian slate
- **Why it's special:** quartzite is metamorphosed sandstone — denser, harder, splits cleaner. Used for premium floors, pool surrounds, exterior paving where you want a modern Italian look.
- **Cost:** $20-35/m³ at quarry, $50-80/m² delivered and cut
- **Suppliers:** small quarries in Caazapá, sold through Construex PY and direct

### 4. **Piedra Laja (Flagstone)** — multiple small quarries
- **Color range:** grey → beige → reddish-brown
- **Texture:** thin (1-3 cm), flat, irregular shapes — the literal "flagstone"
- **Best use:** garden paths, terrace flooring, stepping stones, pool decks
- **Why it's the closest to Dutch flagstone:** this is the actual Spanish-language term "laja" = flagstone. Same use case.
- **Cost:** $8-15/m² at small quarries, $20-35/m² delivered
- **Note:** quality varies wildly between small producers. Visit the quarry, hand-pick the stones, OR work with a consolidator.

## Comparison: PY stone vs Dutch flagstone

| Feature | Dutch flagstone (Leisteen / Natursteen) | PY arenisca | PY piedra laja |
|---|---|---|---|
| Color | Blue-grey, brown-grey, ochre | Beige → ochre → red | Grey → beige → red-brown |
| Hardness | High (slate) | Medium-high (sandstone) | Medium |
| Thickness | 2-4 cm typical | 3-5 cm typical | 1-3 cm typical |
| Cost in NL/PY | €25-50/m² | $25-40/m² (€23-37/m²) | $20-35/m² (€18-32/m²) |
| Availability | Wide range | Wide range (within 50-100 km of LQV) | Variable, visit quarry |
| Workability | Splits easily | Splits easily | Splits easily |
| Frost resistance | High | Medium (test before using in PY winter exposure) | Medium |
| Italian look | No (Northern European) | **YES** | Somewhat |

**The aesthetic difference:** Dutch flagstone is **Northern European cool grey-blue**, PY arenisca is **Southern European warm beige-red**. Both are "natural stone" but they evoke different palettes. For LQV's Italianate eco-retreat positioning, the warm PY stone is actually a better fit.

## What to ask each supplier (the 7 questions)

When Wes calls (or has Sonja call on his behalf — Spanish-language):

1. **"Tienen piedra laja/areisca para revestimiento?"** — Do you have flagstone/sandstone for facing?
2. **"Qué colores tienen disponible?"** — What colors do you have? (Ask for photos of current stock.)
3. **"Cuál es el precio por m² para espesor de 3-5 cm?"** — Price per m² for 3-5 cm thickness?
4. **"Hacen entrega a Escobar, Paraguarí? Cuánto cuesta el flete?"** — Do you deliver to Escobar, Paraguarí? How much is the freight?
5. **"Tienen mínimo de pedido?"** — Minimum order?
6. **"Puedo visitar la cantera y elegir las piedras?"** — Can I visit the quarry and hand-pick stones?
7. **"Tienen referencias de obras similares?"** — References from similar projects?

**Always visit the quarry in person before placing a large order.** Stone varies enormously between batches — the photo on the website may not match what they currently have.

## Pitfalls (real failures to avoid)

1. **Buying stone by photo from a website.** Always visit or send someone to the quarry to see current stock.
2. **Assuming "granite" is the same hardness everywhere.** PY granite varies — some is very hard (Itá), some is softer and weathers quickly (Quiindy). Ask for samples and do a 2-week water-soak test before committing.
3. **Using sandstone in constantly wet locations (pool surrounds, water features).** PY sandstone is moderately porous; it will discolor and eventually degrade if constantly wet. Use **granite or quartzite** for those locations.
4. **Mismatched thickness on floors.** Specify a tolerance (e.g., "3 cm ± 5 mm") or your tiler will spend days leveling.
5. **Cement pointing on flagstone floors.** Cement is too rigid, traps moisture, cracks. **Use sand + lime mortar** for joints, or leave open joints and fill with sand + a polymeric sand stabilizer.
6. **Forgetting about transport weight.** Granite cobblestones weigh ~1.6 tons per m³. A 10 m² cobblestone path = 1.6 tons. Make sure your access road can handle the truck.
