## Why this works (the physics)

A **segmental arch** (low-rise arch, rise ≈ 1/4 to 1/6 of span) transfers vertical load into **horizontal thrust** at the abutments, then into the ground. For a 5 m span, 4 m wide, supporting a Cat 308 (8.2 ton) or a loaded dump truck (6-8 ton):

- **Arch ring thickness:** 30-40 cm (one-and-a-half brick thick)
- **Abutment depth:** 1.2-1.5 m into undisturbed soil or bedrock, with a **footing 60 cm wide × 30 cm deep reinforced concrete**
- **Fill above arch:** rubble stone + compacted earth, **crowned for drainage** (rain runs off, doesn't pool on deck)
- **Parapet walls:** 60-80 cm high stone masonry (the "Italian" look), coped with flat capping stones

The reason this is forgiving for DIY: **arches don't bend.** As long as the abutments don't move and the centering (formwork) is properly built, the stones/concrete lock into a compression-only structure. There is **no tensile stress** to worry about in plain concrete.

## Construction sequence (8-12 weeks for a first-timer crew)

### Phase A — Site prep + abutments (weeks 1-3)
1. Excavate to bedrock or firm clay (refuse = hand-dug hardpan that doesn't crumble). For LQV (Atlantic Forest, clay-loam): expect 1-1.5 m depth.
2. Build **reinforced concrete footings**: 60 cm wide × 30 cm deep × 4.5 m long (one per side), rebar #4 (12 mm) @ 15 cm O.C. both ways. Mix: 1:2:3 (cement:sand:gravel) by volume.
3. Cure 7 days minimum before loading.

### Phase B — Centering (formwork) (week 4)
4. Build **wooden centering** (the curved form the arch is built ON). Use 2-3 layers of 19 mm plywood bent over curved ribs (1×4 lumber cut as arcs). The centering must support the weight of wet concrete + stone until cured — typically 200-300 kg/m² of arch surface.
5. Critical: **strongbacks** every 60 cm, wedged so you can **lower the centering slowly** (de-centering) after 14 days of cure. Jacks or wedges on each strongback.

### Phase C — Arch ring (weeks 5-7)
6. Lay the **voussoirs** (wedge-shaped stones forming the arch). Italian style: **rubble stone facing** (irregular sandstone or local stone from R46 — Piribebuy or Cerro Vera, $12-18/m³) set in lime mortar, with a **concrete hearting** behind/inside.
7. Key stone (the center voussoir) goes in last. After it's placed, the arch is **self-supporting in compression**.
8. Cure 14 days before de-centering.

### Phase D — Spandrels, fill, deck (weeks 8-10)
9. Build **spandrel walls** (the walls above the arch ring, up to deck level) in rubble masonry.
10. Fill the interior with rubble + compacted earth, crowned so water runs off.
11. **Deck surface:** 10 cm reinforced concrete slab (1:2:3 with #3 rebar @ 20 cm O.C.) OR cob + lime plaster for a more natural look (slightly lower load rating — 4-5 ton instead of 8).
12. **Parapet walls** 70 cm high with **capping stones** (the most visible "Italian" detail — flat slab stones laid on top of the parapet, slightly overhanging).

### Phase E — Drainage + curing (weeks 11-12)
13. Install **weep holes** through the spandrels at deck level (every 2 m) so water behind the arch can escape.
14. **Waterproofing:** lime plaster + linseed-oil topcoat on the arch extrados (top surface before fill) — keeps water from saturating the fill.
15. Final cure 28 days before opening to traffic.

## Italian style details (the "look")

- **Voussoirs:** visible, slightly projecting from the arch face (~2-3 cm proud), laid in regular courses. Use **dressed local sandstone** (Cantera Piribebuy) for the exposed face.
- **Parapet coping:** flat slab stones laid as a continuous cap, slightly projecting (~3-4 cm) to shed water. Often called *"testa di ponte"* (bridge head) in Italian.
- **String course:** a horizontal band of dressed stone at deck level, breaking up the parapet visually.
- **Keystone:** the center voussoir slightly emphasized (often a different shape or with a carved date).
- **Pointing:** mortar joints raked back 1-2 cm from the stone face — the depth of shadow makes the stones "pop" visually. **Lime mortar only** (not cement) — cement is too rigid, traps moisture, cracks in 5-10 years.

## Pitfalls (from real Roman + Italian bridge failures)

1. **Abutment rotation.** #1 cause of DIY arch collapse. Fix: dig to **refusal** (clay you can't dig with a shovel), not just to "looks solid." If you hit water, dig deeper.
2. **Centering sag.** Plywood bends under wet concrete weight. Fix: **strongbacks every 50 cm, not 60**. Use 19 mm plywood, not 12 mm.
3. **De-centering too early.** The arch looks solid but mortar is still green. Fix: **14 days minimum, ideally 21**. Lower the wedges **uniformly and slowly** — drop each one 5 mm per day, not all at once.
4. **No weep holes.** Water builds up behind the spandrels, freezes (yes, even in PY winter it gets to -2°C in Escobar), bursts the masonry. Fix: **weep holes every 2 m at deck level.**
5. **Cement pointing on a lime mortar arch.** Differential stiffness causes the pointing to fall out in 3-5 years. Fix: **lime mortar throughout.** If you must use cement for speed, isolate the pointing from the structural mortar.
6. **Live load on a single point.** A 6-8 ton excavator parked dead-center is fine (that's the design load). A 6-8 ton excavator **driving over the edge of the deck** can flip it. Fix: **parapet walls 80+ cm high** (acts as wheel guard) and **fill the deck edge solidly** (no voids near the parapet).
