## TL;DR — Leafcutters are NOT a plague to eradicate. They are the dominant herbivore of every Neotropical forest, including yours. The right strategy is **diversion + protection of priority trees + zero chemical control.**

**The most counter-intuitive but important fact:** leafcutter ants (Atta and Acromyrmex) are **not eating the leaves.** They are **using the leaves as substrate to grow a fungus** (Leucoagaricus gongylophorus), and they eat the fungus. They are **fungus farmers**, not leaf eaters. This matters because:

1. They are **selective** — they prefer certain leaves over others (often the same ones you want to plant).
2. They are **central-place foragers** — they travel up to 200 m from the nest, but they prefer the closest acceptable leaf source. If you give them something they like closer, they take it.
3. They are **extremely difficult to kill with chemicals** because the queen lives 4-6 m underground in a chamber with millions of symbiotic microbes that detoxify most poisons.

**This is why biological control is the only durable answer.**

## Question 1 — What can we do about leafcutter ants biologically?

### The 5-tier biological strategy (in order of escalation)

#### Tier 1 — Accept and redirect (free, do nothing)
**This is the default for the 50+ ha of forest you won't be building on.** Leafcutters are part of the forest. They prune ~10-15% of leaf growth annually in tropical forests, which actually **stimulates forest regeneration** (similar to how grazing stimulates grassland productivity).

**Action:** in zones >50 m from any structure, valuable specimen tree, or garden, do nothing. The forest will balance itself.

#### Tier 2 — Diversion planting (cheap, very effective)
**Plant things they prefer MORE than your valuable plants, placed strategically between the nest and the thing you want to protect.**

The "trap crops" that leafcutters prefer in PY:
- **Citrus** (orange, lemon, grapefruit) — they love citrus leaves
- **Rose bushes** — high-priority
- **Hibiscus** — moderate
- **Bean leaves** (Phaseolus) — moderate
- **Eucalyptus** (coppice regrowth) — high-priority

**Strategy:** plant a hedge of citrus + roses ~10 m upwind of any valuable tree you want to protect, between the most likely nest sites and the tree. The ants hit the citrus first, never reach the protected tree.

**Cost:** ~$50-150 per protection hedge (10 plants @ $5-15 each).

#### Tier 3 — Direct nest intervention (the "biological control" classic)
**Apply entomopathogenic fungi to the nest.** The leafcutter's obligate fungus garden is vulnerable to specific fungi that don't harm other insects:

- ***Beauveria bassiana*** — a fungus that infects the ants themselves, spreads through the colony
- ***Metarhizium anisopliae*** — same idea, different species
- ***Trichoderma*** — a fungus that outcompetes the ants' fungus garden in the lab, but field results are mixed

**How to apply:**
1. Locate the nest (follow the foraging trail backward).
2. Open 5-10 entrance holes with a stick.
3. Pour 1-2 liters of fungal spore suspension into each hole.
4. Best timing: **start of rainy season (October)** when humidity helps the fungus establish.
5. Repeat annually if activity returns.

**Products available in PY:**
- **Bovemax** (Beauveria bassiana-based) — manufactured in Brazil, available at agricultural suppliers in Asunción. ~$20-40 per treatment per nest.
- **Metarril** (Metarhizium) — same source. ~$30-50 per treatment.

**Effectiveness:** 60-80% colony suppression within 4-8 weeks. Not 100% — leafcutters are remarkably resilient.

#### Tier 4 — Biological deterrent barriers (for protecting specific trees)
A combination of plants that repel leafcutters through volatile compounds:

- **Canela (Nectandra spp.)** — native PY tree, leaves contain safrole and other ant-repellent volatiles. Plant as a hedge around the protected tree.
- **Pongamia (Millettia pinnata)** — legume tree with pongamol in the seeds, ant-deterrent. Not native but grows well in PY.
- **Mint family herbs** (orégano, menta, albahaca) — planted around the base of the protected tree, the volatile oils deter foragers.
- **Coffee grounds** spread in a 30 cm radius ring around the trunk — caffeine is toxic to the ants' fungus. Replace monthly.

**Cost:** ~$30-80 per protected tree initial setup, $20/year maintenance.

#### Tier 5 — Physical barriers (last resort for individual trees)
- **Tree wrap:** 50 cm tall aluminum foil or smooth plastic wrap around the trunk, coated with a thin layer of **sticky barrier** (Tanglefoot or similar). Ants cannot climb past the sticky layer.
- **Cost:** $5-10 per tree per year (wrap + barrier renewal).
- **Pitfall:** the sticky barrier also traps beneficial insects. Use only on trees where you cannot tolerate any defoliation.

### Critical: **plant valuable species in the RIGHT location from day one**

Leafcutter pressure varies enormously across a 62-ha property. Some zones will have 5+ nests per hectare; others will have zero. **Use the existing pattern.**

- **Avoid** planting valuable specimens within 30 m of an active Atta sexdens nest.
- **Prefer** planting in zones with Acromyrmex only (less aggressive, easier to manage).
- **Map the nests** during your site visit (W0.3) — a 1-day walk with a GPS recording every nest you find. The pattern tells you where the pressure is.

### Best species to plant in HIGH leafcutter pressure zones

These species are either avoided by leafcutters or recover quickly from defoliation:
- **Lapacho negro** (Handroanthus heptaphyllus) — leafcutters generally avoid (tough leaves, low palatability)
- **Cedro** (Cedrela odorata) — moderately avoided
- **Yvyra pyta** (Peltophorum dubium) — moderately avoided
- **Inga edulis** — FAST regrowth, leafcutters take some leaves but the tree recovers in days
- **Guayubira** (Patagonula americana) — tough leaves, low preference

### Worst species to plant in HIGH leafcutter pressure zones

These are leafcutter favorites — they'll be stripped within hours:
- **Citrus** (use as trap crop, not specimen tree)
- **Rose** (same)
- **Eucalyptus** (coppice regrowth is leafcutter candy — use only in dedicated zones away from specimens)
- **Most fruit trees** (mango, guayaba, citrus)

## What does NOT work (don't waste your money)

1. **Synthetic pyrethroids** (cypermethrin, lambda-cyhalothrin) — kill the foraging ants but not the colony. Within 2 weeks, new foragers emerge. Plus they kill bees and other beneficial insects.
2. **Diazinon, chlorpyrifos, fipronil** — banned or heavily restricted in most countries for good reason. Persistent in soil, toxic to birds, fish, and humans. **Refuse any pest control company that proposes these.**
3. **Boiling water poured into nests** — kills some workers but the queen survives 4-6 m down. Plus it sterilizes the soil around the nest for 6-12 months.
4. **Diesel fuel or kerosene** — illegal, dangerous, kills everything, contaminates groundwater. **Never.**
5. **"Miracle" products from internet** — if it sounds too good to be true, it is. There is no chemical that kills an Atta colony without collateral damage.

## Pitfalls (real failures)

1. **Treating every nest you see.** Counterproductive — you'll never treat all of them, and the survivors expand into the niches you cleared. Focus treatment on nests within 50 m of structures or valuable trees.
2. **Beauveria applied during dry season.** The fungus dies before it establishes. **October only** (start of rains).
3. **Trap crops planted too close to the thing you're protecting.** Leafcutters will eat BOTH if they're within the same foraging range. Keep the trap crop 10+ m away from the protected tree, between the nest and the protected tree.
4. **Sticky barrier on a mature tree with rough bark.** Ants find paths under the bark past the barrier. Only effective on smooth-barked saplings <5 yr old.
5. **Mixing biological and chemical control.** If you spray a pyrethroid AND apply Beauveria, the chemical kills the fungus before it establishes. **Pick one strategy per zone.**

## Integration with R-LQV18 (termites)

**Termites and leafcutters are very different problems requiring very different responses:**
- Termites: structural pests → prevent with barriers, treat with boric acid
- Leafcutters: ecological keystone → manage with diversion + selective protection, not eradicate

**Don't apply termite strategies to leafcutter nests or vice versa.** The biology is completely different.
