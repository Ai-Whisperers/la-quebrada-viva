## Letter 2 — Guaraní version (cultural protocol, opening greeting)

**Mba'éichapa Mbyá kuñakarai, Mbyá kuñahára, ha Mbyá tendota?**

(¿Cómo están las mujeres Mbyá, los hombres Mbyá, y los líderes Mbyá?)

**Che réra Wesley van de Camp, che rembiapo'áva Erebus ndive.**

(Mi nombre es Wesley van de Camp, mi asistente es Erebus.)

**Rohupyty roikove hína peteí tekoha Paraguarí pegua, Escobar rupi, ha rohupyty roñe'ê.**

(Tenemos una propiedad en Paraguarí, por Escobar, y queremos conversar.)

**Che rembiapo niko peteí ñemoaranduha oiko porã ha oñangarekóva yvyatýpe.**

(Mi proyecto es un lugar de descanso y cuidado de la tierra.)

**Roipota roma'ẽ mbo'épa rombyasy, roiporu porãta ha roñe'ẽporãta nderehe.**

(Queremos saber qué piensan, cómo usar bien el lugar, y conversar bien con ustedes.)

**Aipo'ípe:** Mbopicua ha Ybyraty — mba'e itépa pe ñe'ẽva?

(Una pregunta: ¿qué significan realmente las palabras Mbopicua y Ybyraty?)

**Aguÿje ndéve.**

(Gracias a ustedes.)

---

**NOTE TO INTERPRETER**: The above Guaraní is **Avañe'ẽ** (Paraguayan Guaraní), which is the lingua franca. **Mbyá Guaraní is a distinct language** — different vocabulary, different pronunciation, no mutual intelligibility in extended conversation. The interpreter must translate from Avañe'ẽ to Mbyá. **Mbyá greetings differ**: they use "Jahe'ã porã" (good day) instead of "Mba'eichapa." The letter is in Avañe'ẽ for the Mbyá who know it (most do, as a second language); the interpreter handles the Mbyá-specific vocabulary during the visit.

## Meeting Brief — what to cover in the first visit

**Before the visit**:

1. **Send the Spanish letter (Letter 1) to INDI** (Asunción, +595 21 447 220) — wait for INDI's response with a community contact + facilitator name.
2. **Confirm with the interpreter/facilitator** that they speak Mbyá Guaraní (not just Avañe'ẽ). Mbyá is a distinct language.
3. **Prepare gifts** (see Gift Budget below) — wrapped, ready to present at the meeting's opening.
4. **Print and bring**:
   - The Spanish letter (1 copy for INDI, 1 for the community leader)
   - The property cadastral map
   - The project summary (1-page)
   - The archaeological-cultural study (bound, for the community library)
5. **Wes reads** the archaeological-cultural study before the visit — he should be able to answer questions about Mbopicua, Ybyraty, Mbyá culture, and the project scope.

**During the visit** (estimated 2-3 hours):

1. **Opening gift exchange** (10 min). Hand the gifts in order of importance:
   - **First** to the community leader (cacique or similar)
   - **Second** to elders present
   - **Third** to other adults
   - **Last** to children (if appropriate, e.g., small toys)
2. **Greeting + introduction** (15 min). Wesley introduces himself in Spanish (interpreter translates to Mbyá). Explain: "We are the new owners of the land at Mbopicua + Ybyraty. We are not from here. We come to listen and learn."
3. **Tell the story** (20 min). The interpreter tells:
   - Why we are here (eco-retreat, low-impact tourism)
   - What we will build (5 cabins + restaurant in Phase 1, 30 cabins total in Phase 3)
   - What we will NOT do (no clear-cutting, no monoculture, no extraction)
   - What we want from them (cultural guidance, possible employment, possible craft partnership)
4. **Listen** (45 min). The community speaks. **Do not interrupt**. Take notes. **Listen for**:
   - Any sacred sites on or near the property (this is the critical question)
   - Any historical use of the land (hunting grounds, gathering sites, water sources)
   - Any concerns about the project
   - Any questions they have
5. **Ask the open question** (15 min): "Is there anything you would like us to do, or NOT do, on this land?"
6. **Discuss potential collaboration** (20 min). If the community is open:
   - Cultural consultant during design (1-2 days, paid)
   - 1-2 Mbyá team members (forest ranger, guide)
   - Mbyá craft for the gift shop (terms TBD)
   - Mbyá traditional medicine walk (a guided experience)
7. **Closing** (10 min). Wesley thanks the community in Spanish (interpreter translates). Confirm follow-up communication. Confirm next steps.

**After the visit**:

1. **Send a thank-you letter** (in Spanish, with Avañe'ẽ greeting) within 1 week.
2. **Document** the consultation: photos (with permission), notes, signed letter of understanding if any.
3. **Update the project file** with the consultation record + photos + names of community leaders + facilitator name.
4. **If a sacred site is identified**: work with the community to **mark it on the cadastral map** and **exclude it from any construction**. The archaeological survey (R35 drone LiDAR + 1-2 day ground survey, ~$2,500) should include this site.
5. **If the community declines further engagement**: respect that decision. The consultation was the value, not the partnership.
